# Capture Closing Lines At The Close, Not After The Game

Closing lines are snapshotted from the live market in the minutes before first pitch by a short-interval job, and every stored close records **when** it was taken. Post-game backfill remains only as a fallback, and never overwrites a captured snapshot.

**Context:** `backfill-clv` reconstructs closes *after* settlement — a 2026-07-19 pick was backfilled on 2026-07-25. Two failures compound there. **Coverage:** once the game is over the prop market is usually gone, so most picks never receive a close at all; Measured CLV sits at 22% against the 90% CONTEXT.md requires before ROI is mature. **Vintage:** `bettingpros.fetch_offer_ladder` takes no timestamp and exposes no "closing" flag, so on the occasions it does return something for a finished game, nothing distinguishes a true close from a stale or reopened line — it is recorded as "the close" regardless.

This matters more than a data-quality nit because CLV is the project's designated process signal and converges far faster than ROI. Separating a 55% strategy from breakeven on results alone takes ~2,300 bets; CLV answers it in a fraction of that. Unmeasured, that advantage is forfeited and the project is left waiting on ROI, which at ~1 pick/day is effectively never. Coverage is the binding constraint on learning anything here (ADR 0009).

**Considered Options:** keep backfilling and accept low coverage; add a vintage guard to the backfill so at least stale closes are flagged (raises honesty, not coverage); snapshot at a fixed daily time (wrong for a slate spanning 10:40am to 7:10pm); snapshot on a short interval and keep whichever capture lands closest to first pitch.

**Decision:** `tracker.py capture-closes` snapshots the two-way consensus for any *open* pick starting within a 45-minute window, and runs every 15 minutes between 08:00 and 21:00 AZ. Each pass keeps the snapshot **closest to first pitch**, so a pick captured at T-25 is superseded when it is captured again at T-4, and a slate spanning the whole day is covered without a schedule per game. Captures are strictly pre-game: once first pitch passes, the price is no longer a closing line and the pick is skipped.

Every snapshot records `captured_at`, `minutes_before_start`, and a `vintage` of `close` (≤30 min before start) or `pre-close`. A price taken hours early is still recorded — better than nothing — but is never labelled a close, because an opening-ish line masquerading as a closing line is the precise defect this replaces. Only markets priced on **both** sides at our number are stored, since a one-sided price cannot be de-vigged and fabricating the other side would be worse than staying Unmeasured.

`backfill-clv` now prefers captured snapshots and falls back to the live post-hoc fetch only for picks without one, never overwriting a capture.

Runs on the home PC: BettingPros 403s datacenter IPs (ADR 0006), the same constraint that forced the morning picks run off the cloud. The job writes `closing_lines.json` locally and does not commit — at a 15-minute cadence that would be commit spam — so the morning run commits it alongside the CLV backfill it feeds. Nothing in the cloud consumes the file, because backfill is residential-only too.

**Consequences:** CLV becomes measurable for the majority of picks rather than the 22% minority, which is what makes ADR 0009's 100-pick resume condition reachable within months instead of years. Scope is MLB player props — the Primary Edge, and what the offer-ladder endpoint prices two-way; game lines and totals need a different market id and are excluded until there is a reason to add them, so their CLV coverage stays near zero. The benchmark remains BettingPros Consensus, not Pinnacle, which is a materially weaker reference than CONTEXT.md's stated ideal; capturing at the right *time* does not fix capturing from the wrong *book*. A silent failure here degrades coverage without any bet being mis-scored, so the job writes a `.CAPTURE-FAILED` sentinel that the session health check surfaces — the same slow-rot class as the 53-day picks outage, which is why it gets an alarm rather than a log line.
